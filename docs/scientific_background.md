# Scientific background

## Autoimmune disease biology

Central and peripheral tolerance normally constrain autoreactive lymphocytes. Their escape can support autoreactive T-cell help, B-cell maturation, autoantibody production, complement activation and chronic cytokine-driven injury. These contributions vary: synovial inflammation and stromal remodeling are prominent in rheumatoid arthritis (RA); immune-complex and complement-mediated injury are central to many manifestations of systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE); glandular lymphocytic infiltration and systemic features coexist in Sjögren disease. A single pathway model should not be imposed across disorders.

## TNF signaling

TNF engages TNFR1 (TNFRSF1A) and TNFR2 (TNFRSF1B), activating context-dependent NF-κB, MAPK, survival and cell-death programs. In vitro RA synovial work placed TNF upstream of parts of the inflammatory cytokine cascade, while anti-TNF treatment of collagen-induced arthritis supplied in-vivo preclinical evidence. Mouse efficacy did not establish human pharmacokinetics, infection risk or clinical benefit; those required trials. Infliximab and adalimumab neutralize TNF with antibodies, whereas etanercept is a soluble receptor-Fc decoy.

## B-cell-directed therapy

CD20 (MS4A1) is expressed across substantial stages of B-cell maturation but not on most terminal plasma cells. Rituximab and glycoengineered type-II obinutuzumab mediate B-cell depletion. BAFF supports B-cell survival through receptors including BAFF-R; APRIL and BAFF also signal through TACI/BCMA contexts. Ianalumab combines BAFF-R blockade with antibody-dependent depletion. Telitacicept is TACI-Fc and sequesters BAFF and APRIL. These mechanisms can reduce pathogenic humoral activity but also perturb protective immunity.

## IL-17 pathway blockade

IL-17A and IL-17F signal through IL-17 receptor complexes to drive inflammatory transcription, chemokine production and neutrophil-associated responses. Bimekizumab is an antibody that neutralizes both cytokines; sonelokimab is a multivalent VHH-based construct targeting both and containing an albumin-binding domain. Dual targeting does not by itself establish superiority, and interference with IL-17 mucosal defense is clinically relevant.

## FcRn and immunoglobulin recycling

Acidic endosomes permit FcRn (FCGRT with β2-microglobulin) to rescue IgG from lysosomal degradation and return it to circulation. Efgartigimod is an engineered Fc fragment that competes for FcRn, increasing IgG catabolism. It lowers pathogenic and non-pathogenic IgG; it does not stop autoantibody production or deplete B cells.

## Complement inhibition

Classical, lectin and alternative pathways converge on C5 cleavage and terminal complement activation. Ravulizumab binds C5, limiting C5a and membrane-attack-complex generation. Its US indications include AChR-antibody-positive generalized myasthenia gravis. Terminal complement blockade carries a serious meningococcal infection risk and acts downstream of autoantibody generation.

## CD40–CD40L communication

CD40L (CD154), expressed particularly by activated T cells, engages CD40 on B cells and antigen-presenting cells to support activation, class switching and germinal-center responses. Dazodalibep is a non-antibody fusion protein that antagonizes CD40L. Phase 2 Sjögren findings support continued study; they are not regulatory approval or evidence of durable remission.

## Precision immunotherapy

Molecular stratification aims to match heterogeneous tissue biology to therapy. R4RA compared rituximab and tocilizumab after TNF-inhibitor failure; the histology-defined primary comparison was not significant, while a molecular B-cell definition supported a differential signal. In biologic-naive RA, STRAP found that the prespecified binary B-cell-rich/poor classification did not predict differential response among rituximab, tocilizumab and etanercept. Later transcriptomic models are promising research results but require prospective clinical-utility validation.

## Emerging platforms

Nanobodies are compact VHH domains amenable to modular multivalency; bispecifics and engineered fusion proteins can combine binding functions; antigen-specific nanoparticles seek tolerance rather than broad suppression; CAR-T is a cellular therapy, not an antibody drug. Delivery systems and antigen-specific tolerance remain largely preclinical in the contexts discussed. Approved, investigational, preclinical and speculative categories must remain visually and analytically separate.
